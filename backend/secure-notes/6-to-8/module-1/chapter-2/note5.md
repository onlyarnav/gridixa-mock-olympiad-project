# What Is AI Bias?

**AI Bias** happens when an AI system learns **unfair or incorrect patterns from the data it is trained on**.

Remember an important idea from earlier topics:

```
Data + Algorithm → AI Learning
```

If the **data itself contains problems**, the AI may learn those problems.

This can cause the AI to make **unfair or incorrect decisions**.

---

# Why Bias Happens in AI

AI systems depend heavily on the **data used during training**.

If the training data is:

* Unfair
* Incomplete
* Unbalanced
* Biased toward certain groups

then the AI may learn **incorrect patterns**.

The learning process might look like this:

```
Biased Data → Pattern Learning → Biased AI Decisions
```

This does not happen because the AI wants to be unfair.
It happens because the **data taught the AI the wrong lesson**.

---

# Example: Hiring AI System

Imagine a large company that builds an AI system to help **select candidates for a job**.

The company trains the AI using resumes of people who were hired in the past.

But suppose that most of the past hires were **men**.

The training data might look like this:

* Many resumes from men who were hired
* Very few resumes from women

The AI studies this data and tries to detect patterns that lead to successful hiring.

The learning process could become:

```
Past Hiring Data → Pattern Learning → Hiring Prediction
```

However, because the training data is **unbalanced**, the AI might mistakenly learn the rule:

> “People similar to past hires are more likely to be good candidates.”

If the past hires were mostly men, the AI might incorrectly assume that **men are better for the job**.

As a result, the system might **unfairly reject highly qualified women**.

This is an example of **AI bias**.

---

# The Idea of “Garbage In, Garbage Out”

Computer scientists often use a simple phrase:

> **Garbage In, Garbage Out**

This means that if a system receives **poor-quality input**, it will produce **poor-quality output**.

We can represent this idea like this:

```
Bad Data → Bad Learning → Bad Decisions
```

If the data given to an AI system is:

* Biased
* Incomplete
* Incorrect

then the AI will produce **biased or incorrect results**.

---

# Why Humans Must Be Careful

AI systems do not decide what data to use.

**Humans choose the data.**

Therefore, it is the responsibility of scientists, engineers, and companies to make sure the data used for training is:

* Fair
* Balanced
* Accurate
* Representative of different groups

The improved process should look like this:

```
Fair and Balanced Data → Better Learning → Fair AI Decisions
```

Careful data selection helps reduce the chances of **AI bias**.

---

# Real-World Importance

Understanding AI bias is very important because AI systems are used in many important areas, such as:

* Hiring decisions
* Loan approvals
* Medical analysis
* Security systems

If AI systems learn unfair patterns, they could make **harmful decisions that affect many people**.

That is why researchers constantly work on ways to make AI **more fair and responsible**.

# Think Like an AI Engineer

Consider the following situation.

An AI system is trained using **only one type of data** and then starts making unfair decisions.

What is the most likely reason?

A) The computer hardware is broken
B) The training data is biased or incomplete
C) The AI is trying to be unfair on purpose

Correct answer: **B**

AI systems do not choose to be unfair.
They simply learn patterns from the **data provided by humans**.

---

# Key Points to Remember

**AI Bias**

* Occurs when AI learns **unfair patterns from biased data**.

**Cause of Bias**

* AI systems learn from **human-created data**.
* If the data is unfair or incomplete, the AI may learn incorrect patterns.

**Garbage In, Garbage Out**

* Poor-quality data leads to poor-quality results.

**Human Responsibility**

* People must carefully choose and review the data used to train AI systems.
