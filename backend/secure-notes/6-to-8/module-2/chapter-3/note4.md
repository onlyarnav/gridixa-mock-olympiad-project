To better understand how AI agents work in the real world, let us look at two well-known examples. These examples show how different systems use the same logical agent loop to achieve their goals.

# Example 1: The Chess AI

One of the most famous AI agents in history was the chess-playing supercomputer **Deep Blue**.

This powerful computer was designed with one clear goal: **to win games of chess**.

In 1997, it defeated the human World Chess Champion **Garry Kasparov**, demonstrating how powerful AI decision-making could be.

Let us see how this chess AI used the **agent loop**.

### Observe

The AI scans the **64 squares of the digital chessboard** to determine the exact position of every piece.

This observation step gathers information about:

* Black pieces
* White pieces
* Current board positions

This information acts as the **input** for the AI agent.

---

### Decide

The AI then uses its powerful computing system to analyze **millions of possible future moves**.

It calculates which move will give it the best advantage.

For example, it might determine that moving a **knight** could trap the opponent’s queen and create a stronger position.

This decision-making process takes place inside the AI’s **brain**.

---

### Act

Once the best move is chosen, the AI sends a command to the system.

For example:

> Move Knight to E4.

This command becomes the **output**, which changes the state of the chessboard.

After the move is made, the AI waits for the human opponent to respond. Once the opponent moves, the **Observe → Decide → Act loop begins again**.

# Example 2: Video Game Bots (NPCs)

Another common example of AI agents can be found in video games.

In many games, you interact with characters called **NPCs**, which stands for **Non-Player Characters**.

NPCs are characters that are **not controlled by human players**. Instead, they are controlled by AI agent systems.

You may encounter these AI agents in games such as:

* Minecraft
* Fortnite

These AI-controlled characters continuously use the **agent loop** to react to the player’s actions.

---

### Observe

The AI bot constantly gathers information from the game environment.

It may track:

* The player’s position (coordinates in the game world)
* The player’s health level
* Obstacles such as walls or buildings

This information acts as the **input data** for the agent.

---

### Decide

Using this information, the AI bot’s brain chooses the best strategy.

For example:

* If the bot has **full health**, it may decide to **attack the player**.
* If the bot has **low health**, it may decide to **escape and hide**.

The AI analyzes the situation and selects the most effective action.

---

### Act

After deciding what to do, the bot executes the chosen action.

Possible actions include:

* Running toward the player
* Attacking with a weapon
* Hiding behind an obstacle

These actions change the game environment and affect how the player experiences the game.

Once the action is complete, the bot immediately returns to the **Observe step**, continuing the loop.

---

# Why These Examples Matter

These examples show that AI agents are used in many different environments.

Whether the agent is:

* Playing a game of chess
* Controlling a character in a video game
* Driving a car or managing a smart home

The same basic logic always applies:

**Observe → Decide → Act**

This simple cycle allows AI agents to behave intelligently and respond to changing situations.

# Think Like an AI Engineer

Consider the following situation.

An enemy character in a video game detects that the player is nearby, decides to attack, and then runs toward the player.

Which part of the agent loop represents the action of running toward the player?

A) Observe
B) Decide
C) Act

Correct answer: **C**

Running toward the player is the **Act** step because it is the action taken by the AI agent.
