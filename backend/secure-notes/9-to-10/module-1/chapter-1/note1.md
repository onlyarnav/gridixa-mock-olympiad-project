# What is a Neural Network?

A **Neural Network** is a computer system inspired by the **human brain**.

Just like the brain has billions of neurons connected together, a neural network is made of **digital neurons connected in layers**.

These layers help the AI **process information step-by-step** until it can make a decision or prediction.

Think of it like a **factory assembly line**.

- Raw materials enter the factory.
- Workers process them in multiple stages.
- A final product comes out at the end.

A neural network works the same way, but instead of workers, it has **layers of neurons doing mathematical calculations**.

---

# The Three Main Layers of a Neural Network

Every neural network has three important types of layers.

## 1. Input Layer (Where Data Enters)

The **Input Layer** is the first layer of the network.

This is where **raw data enters the system**.

Examples:

- An image recognition AI receives **pixels of an image**.
- A speech recognition AI receives **audio signals**.
- A recommendation system receives **user data**.

### Analogy

Think of the **Input Layer like the entrance gate of a school**.

Students (data) enter through the gate before moving to classrooms (processing layers).

---

## 2. Hidden Layers (The Thinking Layers)

The **Hidden Layers** are where most of the **computation and learning happens**.

These layers perform mathematical transformations on the data to discover patterns.

Modern AI systems use **many hidden layers**, which is why the field is called **Deep Learning**.

### Analogy

Imagine a **team of detectives solving a mystery**.

Each detective looks at different clues and passes their conclusions to the next detective.

Similarly, hidden layers **analyze information step-by-step** to uncover patterns.

---

## 3. Output Layer (The Final Decision)

The **Output Layer** produces the **final prediction or answer**.

Examples:

- Identifying whether an image contains a **cat or dog**
- Predicting whether an email is **spam or not**
- Recommending a **movie you might like**

### Analogy

Think of the **Output Layer like the judge in a courtroom**.

After hearing all arguments (data processing), the judge gives the **final verdict**.

---

# What Exists Inside Each Layer?

Each layer is made of **digital neurons**, also called **nodes**.

These neurons are connected with lines, forming a network.

Every connection has two important numbers:

- **Weight**
- **Bias**

These numbers control how the AI processes information.

---

# Weights (The Importance Score)

A **Weight** tells the AI how important a specific input is.

Some inputs matter more than others when making decisions.

### Analogy

Imagine you are deciding which restaurant to visit.

You might consider:

- Food quality
- Price
- Distance

If food quality is most important to you, you give it **more importance**.

Weights work the same way—they tell the AI **which inputs should matter more**.

---

# Bias (The Baseline)

A **Bias** is an extra number added to the calculation so that a neuron can still activate even if inputs are zero.

It helps the network adjust its decision boundary.

### Analogy

Think of a **bias like the starting score in a video game**.

Even before you collect points, you might start with **10 bonus points**.

Similarly, bias gives the neuron a **starting baseline value**.

---

# Putting It All Together

A neural network works like this:

1. Data enters through the **Input Layer**.
2. The **Hidden Layers** process the data using neurons, weights, and biases.
3. The **Output Layer** produces the final decision.

Each neuron processes information and passes it forward, allowing the network to **learn patterns and make predictions**.

---

# Key Ideas to Remember

- Neural networks are inspired by the **human brain**.
- They are made of **layers of digital neurons**.
- There are **three main layers**:
  - Input Layer
  - Hidden Layers
  - Output Layer
- **Weights** measure the importance of inputs.
- **Bias** provides a baseline so neurons can activate even without input.
